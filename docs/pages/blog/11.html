Devlog #11: Finding My Bearings Again

It’s been a few months since the last update. Life pulled me in a few different directions, and when I did have time to work on Samnite Supply Lines, I found myself a bit disoriented about how to push the MVP forward. But over the past couple of weeks, I’ve finally started to regain momentum — enough that it feels right to share where things stand.

🎨 Title Screen & Game Reset Logic

One of the quieter but important additions is the new title screen. It’s still simple, but it gives the project a proper entry point and, more importantly, a clean way to reset the entire game state when starting a new run. That reset logic has been overdue, and getting it in place makes the whole project feel more like a real game rather than a collection of systems glued together.

🚜 A Big UX Win: “Tell the Unit What You Want First”

The biggest improvement — and the one I’m most excited about — is a new workflow for how Units carry out tasks.

In older classics like Civilization III or Alpha Centauri, you move a unit to a tile and then tell it what to do. That works fine until you have dozens or hundreds of units marching around. By the time a unit reaches its destination, you’re left wondering:
“Why did I send you here again?”

I wanted to avoid that problem entirely.

So now, when you activate a Farmer Unit, you first tell it what you want — for example, “Build a Farm.” Only after choosing the action do you select where you want that Farm built. The Farmer then automatically travels to that hex and performs the action on arrival.

Under the hood, this meant building the basic “Build Farm Where Unit Is” action, then extending it so the player can click any hex and issue a “Go Build Farm Here” command. From there, I added a dedicated input mode so the game knows that your next click isn’t selecting a tile normally — it’s choosing the target for the build order.

It’s a small shift in interaction design, but it dramatically improves clarity and reduces mental load. Units now feel purposeful instead of forgetful.

🌱 Moving Forward

Getting these pieces in place — the title screen, the reset flow, and especially the new Unit‑action workflow — has helped me reorient myself. I’m feeling more grounded in the direction of the MVP again, and I’m excited to keep building from here.

Keep checking back for more updates; I’ll keep sharing as work progresses.
